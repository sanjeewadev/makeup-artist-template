# Artist Brand, Trust & Social Proof

## Goal

Chat 11 makes the artist herself a stronger part of the product.

The website should answer:

1. Who is this artist?
2. What does she care about in the work?
3. What evidence supports that point of view?
4. What do real clients say about the experience?
5. Where can I see more current work?
6. How do I know the business is reachable and real?

The answer must come from authentic content—not fabricated status signals.

## Trust principle

Premium trust is built from:

- real artist identity
- strong real work
- real service context
- real client testimonials
- real contact details
- real social profiles
- useful, verifiable credentials
- consistent point of view

Do not add decorative “featured in” logos, awards, client counts, star ratings, or press claims unless the client can genuinely support them.

## About page role

The About page is now the main trust center.

It combines:

- artist identity
- artist portrait
- service area
- tagline
- short brand description
- direct enquiry information
- social profile signal
- personal story
- Portfolio evidence
- credentials
- Services
- client testimonials
- final availability CTA

It should feel like an artist profile, not a résumé.

## Hero identity

The About hero uses:

- About eyebrow
- About heading
- About lead
- artist portrait
- artist name
- tagline
- service area

Use a real artist portrait or artist-at-work image for client launch.

Do not use stock photography as the real artist identity.

## Trust strip

The About page can surface genuine identity signals such as:

- personal approach / site description
- primary service area
- direct enquiry email
- first real social profile

These should disappear naturally when the source data does not exist.

## Point of view

The About Story should explain:

- what the artist notices
- what matters during the experience
- how the work should feel
- relevant background
- why the approach is personal

First-person writing is encouraged when it sounds natural.

Avoid generic phrases such as “passionate about making people beautiful” unless they genuinely add something specific.

## Portfolio evidence

About uses published Portfolio projects as visual proof.

The principle is:

**say less, show the work.**

Selected projects demonstrate that the stated point of view exists in practice.

Real published projects always take priority over demo projects.

## Credentials

Credentials are optional.

Useful credibility details include:

- real training
- genuine specialisms
- verifiable awards
- relevant professional roles
- meaningful experience
- real industry education

Avoid:

- invented press
- fake awards
- fabricated years of experience
- unsupported client counts
- fabricated celebrity/client names
- arbitrary statistics

If there are no meaningful credentials, it is better to omit the section.

## Practice / Services

The About page links directly into real Services.

This connects personal brand with commercial offering without turning the profile into a sales page.

## Social profiles

Real Site Settings social links are surfaced in:

- Footer
- About trust strip
- About social-profile chapter

Only add profiles the artist actively wants clients to visit.

Do not add empty or abandoned networks merely to create more icons.

## Testimonials

Testimonials are trust evidence, not marketing copy.

Each testimonial may display:

- real client name or approved attribution
- client’s own words
- linked Service
- location
- permitted portrait

Service context is now surfaced publicly when available.

### Testimonial rules

- use the client’s own words
- light trimming is acceptable
- do not rewrite the meaning
- do not create synthetic praise
- do not present AI-generated copy as a client quote
- use client portraits only with permission
- link a Service when known

## Homepage trust

The Homepage Client Notes chapter also surfaces linked Service context where available.

This keeps social proof consistent across:

- Home
- About
- Services

## Footer identity

The footer now prefers real Site Settings content:

- artist/business name
- tagline
- short description
- direct contact
- service area
- social profiles

This makes the global site shell reinforce the same brand rather than reverting to generic template language.

## CMS content guidance

### Site Settings

Treat Site Settings as the source of truth for identity.

#### Artist name

Use the exact professional name the artist wants clients to see.

#### Tagline

Use a short, ownable line.

Avoid vague luxury wording that could belong to any artist.

#### Short description

Use two or three concise sentences explaining:

- type of work
- point of view
- practice

This content may appear in high-visibility global brand surfaces.

### About

Keep the story personal and specific.

### Testimonials

Keep attribution and wording authentic.

## Demo boundary

Demo content exists to make the commercial template presentation-ready.

It must not be represented as the real artist’s:

- identity
- clients
- reviews
- awards
- work
- professional history

Before real launch:

1. replace demo artist imagery
2. write the real artist story
3. add genuine credentials only
4. publish real Portfolio projects
5. publish real Services
6. add genuine client reviews
7. add real social profiles
8. confirm all client portraits/photos have permission
9. confirm direct contact information
10. review every public claim for accuracy

## Commercial principle

A premium beauty website should not need to shout:

**“Trust me.”**

The work, identity, consistency, client experience, and real evidence should make the conclusion obvious.
